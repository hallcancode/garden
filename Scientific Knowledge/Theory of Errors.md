The [[Scientific Method#Theories|theory]] of errors describes how to measure the accuracy and precision of a measurement.

Random errors are harder to detect and fix than systematic ones.   |

Mistakes typically show up as outliers, which should be removed before any kind of statistical analysis is preformed.  Generally, there are three sources of error:
* Natural error
* Instrumentation
* Personal
The first two are systematic forms of error, and the final typically appears as a random form of error.  No [[Scientific Method#Observations|observation]] is ever exact, and all values measured have some level of error.  

The most probable value is given by the mean of all measurements taken.  From that point we can calculate the *residual*, or the difference between the mean and observed value.  We may assume that the residual of our measurements follows a normal distribution, meaning that a large residual is less probable than a smaller one.  

The standard deviation defined the width of distribution which describes the residuals.  The percent error describe the probablility of a given error occuring.
