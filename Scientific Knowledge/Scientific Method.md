The scientific method for Chemistry is nearly identical as the scientific method outside of Chemistry.  The scientific method consists of the following components:

## Experiments
*Experiments*, which are collections of observations, that lead to hypothesizes.

## Observations
*Observations*, are the quantitative and qualitative raw data the may be represented through graphs and tables.  Verified through several samples, "duplicating," which should occur *at least* 3 times, although the more the better.

## Hypothesis
*Hypothesis*, which is some potential, educated, predication as to what observations may be found. Hypothesizes are potential outcomes based of evidence, in such a way that they are testable and falsifiable.  Hypothesizes are an interpretation of the observations that we have already collected.

## Theories
*Theories* are explanation that are unlikely to be changed by further experimentation.  Comes from an abundant amount of evidence that are discovered over time.  Theories allow us to make predictions of the unobserved and, despite the popular usage of the word, are not guesses.

## Laws
*Laws* are constant observations that can not be broken.  Laws have a strong foundation of understanding and explanation behind them.
