*Friction* is defined as a [[Introduction to Classical Mechanics#Force|force]] that resists the slipping of the body relative to another.  The *frictional force* acts parallel to a contacting surface in the direction opposing the relative motion, or the force causing a tendency for motion.  There are two overarching types of friction: 

 + Static friction, $F_s$,  which is the force that acts when the object remains at rest.
 + Kinetic friction, $F_k$, which acts when the object moves.

If, at some point, a force is exerted greater than the maximum force of static friction, then the object will enter motion, where kinetic friction will resist the motion of that object.   

*Dry friction* occurs when there is no lubricating fluid altering the contacting of two surfaces.  

## Static Friction
The force of *static friction* is the frictional force that acts upon an object when the object remains in equilibrium.  Static friction $\vec{F_s}$ is given by the expression
$$
F_s \lt \mu_s F_n
$$
Where $\mu_s$ is the coefficient of static friction and $\vec{F_n}$ is the normal force exerted upon the body.  The point at which $F_s = \mu_s F_n$ is the point of *impending motion*, also known as the *edge of motion*.  

### Determining the Coefficient of Static Friction
The angle $\theta$ at which an object begins to slide against some material gives the coefficient of static friction $\mu_s$, such that:
$$
\mu_s = \tan\theta
$$
The coefficient of static friction greatly depends on the materials of the two objects, 

| Contact materials    | Coefficient of Static Friction |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Metal on ice         | 0.03 - 0.05                    |
| Wood on wood         | 0.30 - 0.70                    |
| Leather on wood      | 0.20 - 0.50                    |
| Leather on metal     | 0.30 - 0.60                    |
| Aluminum on aluminum | 1.10 - 1.70                    |
