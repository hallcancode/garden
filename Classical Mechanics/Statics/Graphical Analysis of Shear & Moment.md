The rate at which the shear $V$ changes, or the slope of Shear-Position Diagram, is relative to the position on the structure is given by:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} V}{\mathrm{d} x} = w\left(x\right)
$$
Where $w$ is a function of position, that gives the loading at that point.  Similarly, the slope of the [[Moment|moment]]-[[Introduction to Rectilinear Kinematics#Position|position]] graph is given by the shear at that point:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} M}{\mathrm{d}x} = V\left(x\right)
$$
For an instant change in [[Momentum|momentum]] $\Delta M$ and shear $\Delta V$ when a moment couple $M_O$ or point loading $F$ is exerted on the structure is given by:
$$
\Delta V = F \qquad \Delta M = M_O
$$
When the shear and moment are results of non-instant changes (i.e., a distributed loading) the changes are instead given by the integrals:
$$
\Delta V = \int w\left(x\right) \cdot \mathrm{d} x \qquad \Delta M = \int V\left(x\right) \cdot \mathrm{d}x
$$
