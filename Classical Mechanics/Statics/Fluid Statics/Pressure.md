Pressure $P$ is the amount of force per an area exerted on an object, thus, mathematically, pressure is inversely related to the force $F$ and the area $A$:

$$
P=\frac{F}{A}
$$

Within Physics, pressure is measured using Pascals (Pa), which are equivalent to N/m<sup>2</sup>, whereas, within Chemistry, the measurement is Kilopascals.

## Types of Pressure
### Gauge Pressure
Gauge pressure is the pressure measured relative to atmospheric pressure. It is relative to the [[Classical Mechanics/Statics/Fluid Statics/Density|density]] $\rho$, the depth of the object $h$, and the [[Gravity#Gravity on Earth|gravitational field strength]] $g$, such that:

$$
P=\rho{g}{h}
$$

As the name implies, gauge pressure is the amount of pressure read by a pressure gauge.
### Absolute Pressure
Absolute pressure is the total amount of pressure on a particular object. It is the sum of the absolute pressure $P_0$ and the gauge pressure, such that:

$$
P=P_0+\rho{g}{h}
$$

## Difference in Pressure
The difference in pressure $\Delta{P}$ at two points is known as:

$$
\begin{align}
\Delta{P}&=p{g}{h_0}-p{g}{h_f}\\
&=pg\left(h_0-h_f\right)\\
&=pg\Delta{h}\\
&=pgd
\end{align}
$$

Where $P$ is the density of the fluid, $g$ is the gravitational field strength, $h_0$ is the initial depth, $h_f$ is the final depth, and $\Delta{h}$ and $d$ is the difference in depth (or distance between the two points).

## Partial Pressures
Within a mixture of gasses, each gas has a *partial pressure*, which is the amount of pressure that that gas was the only gas present within the container.  The Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures states that the total sum of each partial pressure, $P_n$ for $n$ gasses, is equal to the pressure of the mixture $P$, such that:
$$
\begin{align}
P &= P_{1} + P_{2} + P_{3} + \dots + P_{n} \\
&= \sum^{n}_{i=1} P_{i}
\end{align}
$$
