The buoyant force ($F_b$) is the [[Introduction to Classical Mechanics#Force|force]] exerted by a fluid on an object that displaces the fluid. The buoyant force is proportional to the [[Classical Mechanics/Statics/Fluid Statics/Density|density]] of the fluid $\rho$, the volume of the fluid displaced $V$, and the [[Gravity|gravitational field strength]] $g$, such that:
$$
F_b=\rho{V}g
$$
Thus, the buoyant force is also equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.

## Relationship to Pressure
The buoyant force is also equivalent to the difference in [[Classical Mechanics/Statics/Fluid Statics/Pressure|pressure]] between the bottom and top faces of an object, such as a cube, in a way that:
$$
\begin{align}
	F_b&=A\Delta{P}\\
	&=pg\ell{A}\\
	&=pg\ell^3\\
	&=pgV
\end{align}
$$
Where $\ell$ is the length of a side, $A$ is the area of a face, and $V$ is the volume of the cube. The difference in pressure between the bottom and top face is the cause of the buoyant force, that is, more pressure pushing awards than is pushing downwards.
