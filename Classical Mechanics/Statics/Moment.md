A *moment* $M$, or a *torque* $\tau$, is a body's tendency to rotate when a [[Introduction to Classical Mechanics#Force|force]] is exerted along some distance.  The magnitude of a moment $M$ is proportional to the magnitude of the force perpendicular $F_\perp$ and the moment arm $d$, such that:
$$
M =  d F_\perp
$$
Where the moment arm is the perpendicular distance at which the force is applied.  This force can be decomposed into its directions along a two-dimensional plane, such that:
$$
M= F_x y + F_y x
$$
The two-dimensional moment is a scalar quantity.  

In [[Introduction to Three Dimensional Systems|three-dimensional space]], moment is a vector quantity, represented by the cross product of the distance between the point and force.  For an instance, the moment $\vec{M_O}$ is proportional to the distance between the origin and point $A$ where a force $\vec{F}$ is applied, such that:
$$
\vec{M}_O = \vec{F} \times \vec{r}_{OA}
$$
