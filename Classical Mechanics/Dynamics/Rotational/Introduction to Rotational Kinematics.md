Rotational kinematics is the study of a particle’s motion around a fixed point without consideration for the forces causing the motion.  Most rotational kinematics follows the same rules as [[Introduction to Rectilinear Kinematics|linear kinematics]], but through rotational analogues.  

## Angular Position
Angular position, $\theta$, is the current location of a particle relative to a fixed point, typically $0$ radians, around another fixed point.  Angular position is relative to the initial position $\theta_0$, initial velocity $\omega$, and acceleration $\alpha$, such that:
$$\theta=\theta_0+\omega_0t+\frac12\alpha{t}^2$$

## Angular Displacement
Angular displacement, notated with the symbol $\Delta\theta$, is the rotational analogue of [[Introduction to Rectilinear Kinematics#Displacement|displacement]], more specifically it is described as a particle’s change in angular position.  Angular displacement is the measurement of the angle, in radians, for which a particle rotates around a specific point.  Thus, angular displacement is proportional to the starting position, $\theta_0$, and current position, $\theta$: 
$$\Delta\theta=\theta-\theta_0$$
The angular displacement is expressed in terms of linear displacement, $s$, and the distance from the fixed point of which the particle rotates around, the radius $r$:
$$s=r\theta$$

## Angular Velocity
Angular velocity, $\omega$, is the rate at which the angular position changes in respect to time.  Thus, the average angular velocity $\bar{\omega}$ is proportional to the angular displacement and change in time $\Delta{t}$, such that:
$$\bar\omega=\frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta{t}}$$
Therefore, angular velocity is measured in radians per a second.  Thus, the instantaneous angular velocity is proportional to the displacement $d\theta$ and time $dt$, such that:
$$\omega=\frac{d\theta}{dt}$$
The angular velocity can be expressed in terms of tangential linear velocity, $v$, and the radius of the system, $r$, such that:
$$v=r\omega$$

## Angular Acceleration
Angular acceleration, $\alpha$, is the rate at which the angular velocity is the rate at which the angular velocity change in respect to time.  The average angular acceleration $\bar\alpha$ is related to:
$$\bar\alpha=\frac{\Delta\omega}{\Delta{t}}$$
Thus, angular acceleration is measured in radians per a second per a second.  Similarly, the instantaneous angular velocity is:
$$\alpha=\frac{d\omega}{dt}$$

The angular acceleration can be expressed in terms of tangential acceleration, $a$, and the radius of the rotational system, $r$, such that:
$$a=r\alpha$$
