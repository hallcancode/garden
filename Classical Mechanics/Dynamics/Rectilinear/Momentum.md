Momentum $p$ is a property of a body's motion which describes how difficult it is to stop, the symbol $p$ comes from the latter word for push - or drive. Momentum is proportional to an object's mass $m$ and velocity $v$ in such a way that:

$$
p=mv
$$

Momentum describes the net force exerted upon a body over time, such that:

$$
\sum{F}=\frac{\Delta{p}}{\Delta{t}}
$$

Thus, the rate of change of momentum $p^\prime$ also describes the net force $\sum{F}$ exerted upon the body.

---

[[Newton's Laws of Motion#Newton's Second Law|Newton's second law]] applies to momentum in such a way that:

$$
\frac{dp}{dt}=\sum{F}=ma=m\frac{\Delta{v}}{\Delta{t}}
$$

Additionally, the impulse-momentum theory applies:

$$
\Delta{p}=J=\Delta{vm}=F\Delta{t}
$$

Where $J$ is the impulse acted on the system.
