Energy, $E$, is an object’s capacity to preform [[Classical Mechanics/Work|work]], and it exists in many forms.  Energy is measured in joules, which is the measurement of how much energy is required to accelerate a 1 kilogram mass in the direction applied ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$), and is a conserved quantity.  Importantly, energy can not be observed directly, instead we measure and observe it through the behaviors and outcomes of the different types of energy.  

## Types of Energy
There are several common forms of energy, each with their own potential variants as well. 

### Kinetic Energy
This type of energy that an object possess due to its [[Motion|motion]].  Kinetic energy $E_k$ is proportional to the [[Mass|mass]] $m$ and velocity $v$ of an object, such that:
$$
E_k=\frac12mv^2
$$
This also applies to fluid dynamics.  Kinetic energy can also be derived through Calculus, such that:
$$
E_k=\int_{v_0}^{v_f} \mathbf{p} \, d\mathbf{v}
$$
Where $v_0$ and $v_f$ are the initial and final velocities of the object respectively, $\mathbf{p}$ is the [[Momentum]] of the object, and $\mathbf{v}$ is the velocity.  The kinetic energy of a rational system is in two parts, one for the tangential motion, which is identical to the first equation, and another for the rotational kinetic energy, which is proportional to the [[Moment of Inertia|inertia]] $I$ and [[Introduction to Rotational Kinematics#Angular Velocity|angular velocity]] $\omega$, such that:
$$
E_r=\frac12I\omega^2
$$
