
## Pressure-Volume Work
*Pressure-volume work* is the [[Work|work]] done onto a system, typically by a [[Introduction to Gases|gas]], due to an external [[Pressure|pressure]] causing a change in volume.  Pressure-volume work $W$ is proportional to the pressure $P$ and $\Delta W$, such that:
$$
W = P \Delta V
$$
Pressure volume work may also be used to describe the work done by a gas.  In these cases, it is the opposite of the work done onto the system, such that:
$$
W=-P \Delta V
$$

## Enthalpy
*Enthalpy* is a descriptive quantity to describe the energy involved in a chemical reaction.  Enthalpy $H$ is proportional to the total energy contained within the system $E$, pressure $P$, and volume $V$, such that:
$$
H = E + PV
$$

## Enthalpy & Reactions
Enthalpy is also a state function, meaning that the change in enthalpy $\Delta H$ is equal to the difference in final and inital enthalpy values, $H_f$ and $H_0$ respectively, where:
$$
\Delta H = H_{f} - H_{0}
$$
When chemical changes occur at constant pressure we can use a simplified form.  
$$
\begin{align}
\Delta H &= H_{f} - H_{0} \\
&= \Delta \left( E + PV \right) \\
&= \Delta E + \Delta PV \\
&= \Delta E + P \Delta V \\
&= q + W - W \\
&= q
\end{align}
$$
Thus, in a reaction where pressure remains constant, the change in enthalpy is simply the heat transfered.
