A parallelogram is a shape in $\mathbb{R}^2$ formed by four verticies with two sets of parallel sides.  The verticies that form a particular parallelogram can be placed within [[Introduction to Three Dimensional Systems|three-dimensional space]] to form a parallelogram within $\mathbb{R}^3$.  

The area of a parallelogram placed within a three-dimensional space is given by the magnitude of the [[Vector Cross-Product|cross product]] between two vectors representing the sides between a shared point. That is, if $P$, $Q$, $R$, and $S$ are points that form a parallelogram in $\mathbb{R}^3$, then, if $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ and $\overrightarrow{PR}$ are the side lengths joined by the point $P$, then:
$$
A = |\overrightarrow{PQ} \times \overrightarrow{PR}|
$$
Where $A$ is the total area formed by the shape.
