Lines in $\mathbb{R}^2$ are formed with a point on the graph, traditionally the $y$-intercept, and the slop of the line.  Similarly, lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ also need a starting point, however, they more specifically need a *direction* for the line to travel in.  Each point of a [[Introduction to Three Dimensional Systems|three-dimensional]] line is given by the [[Introduction to Vector Functions|vector function]]:
$$
\overrightarrow{p}\left(t\right) = \overrightarrow{r_0} + t\overrightarrow{v}
$$
Where $\overrightarrow{p}$ is the point at position $t$ directed along $\overrightarrow{v}$ from $\overrightarrow{r_0}$.  Lines can also be represented by a symmetric equation, which removes the position variable ($t$) from the equation:
$$
\frac{x-x_0}{a} = \frac{y-y_0}{b} = \frac{z-z_0}{c}
$$
Where $<a,b,c>$ is the vector parallel to the line $\left(x_0,y_0,z_0\right)$.  Two lines within the same three-dimensional space must either: (1) be parallel, that is they align with each other, (2) intersect, that is they share some point, or (3) skew, they are neither parallel nor do they intersect with one another.  

Line segments are paths in the shape of a line of the points between two other points.  That is, they are non-infinite lines.

## Planar Intersections
[[Planes]] intersect with a given point when the values of the line match a possible solution for the plane.  That is, when the line's $x,y,z \in \mathbb{R}$ values solve a plane of the form $ax + by + cz + d = m$.  

>[!example] Problem 46, ch. 12§5 (Stewart, Clegg, Watson's Calculus, 9 ed.)
>**Question.** Find the point at which the line intersects with the given plane.  
>$$
>x = t-1 \quad y = 1+2t \quad z = 2-t; \quad 3x - y + 2z = 5
>$$
