A sphere, literally "ball" in Ancient Greek, is a [[Introduction to Three Dimensional Systems|three-dimensional]] shape comprised of points equidistant some distance, the radius; $r$, from the center of the shape.  As such, the sphere is analogous to a [[Circle|circle]] in $\mathbb{R}^3$.  The equation of any sphere is given by its central point $C=P\left(x_0, y_0, z_0\right)$ and radius $r$, such that:
$$
r^2 = 
\left(x-x_0\right)^2 +
\left(y-y_0\right)^2 +
\left(z-z_0\right)^2
$$

## Planar Intersections of a Sphere
For each primary plane, that is the $x$-$y$, $x$-$z$, and $y$-$z$ planes, a sphere may intersect where the value that remains constant throughout the plane is set to its constant value, i.e., zero.  That is:
$$
\begin{align}
r^2 &= \left(x-x_0\right)^2 + \left(y-y_0\right)^2 + \left(0-z_0\right)^2, \text{ for the }x\text{-}y\text{ plane.} \\
&= \left(x-x_0\right)^2 + \left(0-y_0\right)^2 + \left(z-z_0\right)^2, \text{ for the }x\text{-}z\text{ plane.} \\
&= \left(0-x_0\right)^2 + \left(y-y_0\right)^2 + \left(z-z_0\right)^2, \text{ for the }y\text{-}z\text{ plane.} \\
\end{align}
$$

>[!example] Example
>**Question.** Given the following equation of a sphere:
>$$10^{2}=\left(x-3\right)^{2}+\left(y+11\right)^{2}+\left(z-6\right)^{2}$$
>Write an equation for where this sphere intersects with the $x$-$y$ plane.  
>
>**Answer.** 
>$$
\begin{align}
10^{2}&=\left(x-3\right)^{2}+\left(y+11\right)^{2}+\left(0-6\right)^{2} \\
&=\left(x-3\right)^{2}+\left(y+11\right)^{2}+\left(0-6\right)^{2} \\
100&=\left(x-3\right)^{2}+\left(y+11\right)^{2}+36 \\
64&=\left(x-3\right)^{2}+\left(y+11\right)^{2} \\
8^2&=\left(x-3\right)^{2}+\left(y+11\right)^{2} \\
\end{align}
$$
