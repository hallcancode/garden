Three-dimensional systems, within the $\mathbb{R}^3$ dimension are comprised of points plotted across three axes.  Thus, any dimensional position or point can be expressed with $3$ numbers.  That is, some point is given by $P\left(a,b,c\right)$, where $a$ is the position along the $x$-axis, $b$ is the position along the $y$-axis, and $c$ is the position along the $z$-axis.  

The set of all real numeral triples can represent all possible points within three dimensional space that is:
$$
\mathbb{R} \times
\mathbb{R} \times
\mathbb{R} =
\left\{
  \left(x,y,z\right) |
  x,y,z \in \mathbb{R}
\right\}
$$
Hence why the symbol $\mathbb{R}^3$ is used to denote three dimensional space.  

## Distance Formula
The distance, $d$, between two points $P_1$ and $P_2$ positioned at $P\left(x_1, y_1, z_1\right)$ and $P\left(x_2, y_2, z_2\right)$ respectively is given by the square root of the sum of squared differences in each dimension.  This is called the *distance formula* and is mathematically expressed as:
$$
d\left(P_1, P_2\right) = \sqrt{\left(x_1 - x_2\right)^2 + \left(y_1-y_2\right)^2+\left(z_1-z_2\right)^2}
$$

## Surface & Solids
Whereas the relation between an $x,y$ pair in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a curve, the relation between an $x,y,z$ pair in $\mathbb{R}^3$ represents a *surface*.  For an example $x=2$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a horizontal line, but in $\mathbb{R}^3$ the same expression represents a plane.
