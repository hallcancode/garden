The *vector cross-product* is an operation between two vectors that produces a third perpendicular to the two inputs.  The direction of the cross-product is given by the right-hand rule.  Its magnitude, $|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|$, is proportional to the magnitude of the two vectors, $|\vec{a}|$ and $|\vec{b}|$, and the $\sin$ of the angle between them, $\theta$, such that:
$$
|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}| \sin\theta
$$
The resultant vector is also given by the expression
$$
\begin{align}

\vec{a} \times \vec{b} &=

\left|\begin{array}.
\hat{\mathbf{i}} &\hat{\mathbf{j}} &\hat{\mathbf{k}} \\
a_x &a_y &a_z \\
b_x &b_y &b_z
\end{array}\right| \\

&
\begin{array}.
= \left(a_y b_z - a_z b_y\right) \hat{\mathbf{i}} \\
- \left(a_x b_z - a_z b_x\right) \hat{\mathbf{j}} \\
+ \left(a_x b_y - a_y b_x\right) \hat{\mathbf{k}} \\
\end{array}

\end{align}
$$
