The *tangent* vector of a [[Limits of Vector Functions|vector function]], $\vec{r}\left(t\right)$, is given by the expression:
$$
\vec{T}\left(t\right) = \frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}}{\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|}
$$
The *normal vector* tells us what direction the curve is turning, and forms a perfect right angle with the tangent vector, it is defined as:
$$
\vec{N}\left(t\right) = \frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{T}}{\mathrm{d}t}}{\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{T}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|}
$$

The *binormal* vector is the cross between the tangent and normal vector:
$$
\vec{B}\left(t\right) = \vec{T}\left(t\right) \times \vec{N}\left(t\right)
$$
These vectors are commonly called the tangential, normal, binormal (TNB) basis.
