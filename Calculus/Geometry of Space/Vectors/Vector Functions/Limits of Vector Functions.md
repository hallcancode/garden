The limit of a vector function is defined as the lime of each component.  For an instance, the limit of a vector function $\vec{r}\left(t\right)$ as the position $t$ approaches some point $a \in \mathbb{R}$ is defined, such that:
$$
\lim_{t \to a} \vec{r}\left(t\right) =
\left\langle
 \lim_{t \to a} r_x\left(t\right),
 \lim_{t \to a} r_y\left(t\right),
 \lim_{t \to a} r_z\left(t\right)
\right\rangle
$$
Thus a vector function is *continuous* if, and only if:
$$
\lim_{t \to a} \vec{r}\left(t\right)
= \vec{r}\left(a\right)
\iff
\vec{r}\left(t\right)
\text{ is continuous at }
t=a
\text{.}
$$
Which may be expanded to infer that:
$$
\lim_{t \to a} \vec{r}\left(t\right) \implies
\begin{cases}
	\lim_{t \to a} r_x\left(t\right) = r_x\left(a\right) \\
	\lim_{t \to a} r_y\left(t\right) = r_y\left(a\right) \\
	\lim_{t \to a} r_z\left(t\right) = r_z\left(a\right) \\
\end{cases}
$$
All vector functions could be considered a form of curve, similar to [[Perametric Functions|perametric functions]], where most are not straight [[Lines|lines]].
