cThe arc length $\ell$ of some [[Limits of Vector Functions|vector function]] $\vec{r}\left(t\right)$ curve along the interval $[a,b]$ is given by the expression:
$$
\ell = \int_a^b \left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|
\cdot \mathrm{d}t
$$
This derivation is founded in the [[Calculus/Integrals/Applications of Integration/Average Value of a Function|mean value theorem]].  Notice how this matches the formula for the [[Introduction to Rectilinear Kinematics#Distance|distance]] of a particle in rectilinear space.  

The *arc length function*, $s\left(t\right)$, defined for some interval $[t_0,t]$ of some vector function $\vec{r}\left(t\right)$ is given by the integral:
$$
s\left(t\right) = \int_{t_0}^t \left|\vec{r}\left(u\right)\right| \cdot \mathrm{d}u
$$
