Minima and maxima are special behaviors of [[Introduction to Functions|functions]], with two types: local and absolute.  Absolute minima and maxima are the lowest, or highest, value within the codomain, or range, of that function’s output.  Local minima and maxima occur at critical points, or points where the [[Introduction to Derivates|derivative]] of the function are equal to $0$ or $\infty$, thus, for some function $f\left(x\right)$ there may exist some collection of points $c$ such that:
$$f^\prime\left(c\right)=0$$
Then, there exists maxima for every value of $x=c$ if, and only if, $f^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)<0$.  Additionally, there exists a minima for every value of $x=c$ if, and only if, $f^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)>0$.  If $f^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)=0$ then we say that there is an “inflection point” at $x$.
