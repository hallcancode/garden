L'Hôpital's rule is a mathematical theory that aids in evaluating certain [[Introduction to Limits|limits]]. L'Hôpital's rule describes that for two functions $f\left(x\right)$ and $g\left(x\right)$, which are differentiable over an interval which contains - but may exclude - $h$, if the limit of $f\left(x\right)$ and $g\left(x\right)$ both approach either $0$ or $\infty$ the limit of the inverse relationship of the two equations is equivalent to the limit of the inverse relation of the [[Introduction to Derivates|derivative]] of both equations, such that: 

$$
\lim_{x\to{h}}\frac{f\left(x\right)}{g\left(x\right)}=\lim_{x\to{h}}\frac{f^\prime\left(x\right)}{g^\prime\left(x\right)}\iff{g}\left(h\right)=f\left(h\right)=\left({0}\vee{\infty}\right)
$$


> [!info] Indeterminate Forms
> Indeterminate forms by exponentials:
> $$0^0 \qquad 1^\infty \qquad \infty^0$$
> Subtraction:
> $$\infty - \infty$$
> Multiplication:
> $$0 \infty$$
> Division:
> $$\frac{0}{0} \qquad \frac{\infty}{\infty}$$
