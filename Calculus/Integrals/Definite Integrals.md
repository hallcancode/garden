The definite [[Introduction to Antiderivatives|integral]] of a [[Introduction to Functions|function]] on some interval is called the definite integral.  For any function $f$, the definite integral $A$ on the interval $\left[x_0,x_1\right]$ is defined as:
$$
A=\int_{x_0}^{x_1}f\left(x\right) \, dx
$$
The definite integral provides the “net” area, that is, if the curve is below the value of $0$, then the area of the function while it is below the curve is negative.  


> [!Example] Example #1
> **Question.** Suppose that $v\left(t\right)$ gives our velocity $v$ at a time $t$, what are the units of our integral?
> **Solution.**  If $v\left(t\right)$ is measured in [[SI Units|SI units]], then velocity would be measured in meters per a second (m/s), then we have:
> $$
> s\left(t\right) = \int v\left(t\right) \, dt
> $$
> The time integral of velocity is displacement, thus our unit for the integral will be meters (m).
