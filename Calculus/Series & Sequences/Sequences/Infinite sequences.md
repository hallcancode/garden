*Infinite sequences* are ordered lists of numbers that follow the pattern:
$$
\left\{a_n\right\}_{n=1}^\infty = \left\{a_1,a_2,a_3, \dots, a_n\right\}
$$
If this sequence gets close to some number as $n$ gets very large, then we say that the sequence is “convergent.”  However, if the sequence is unbounded, then we say it is “[[Convergence#Divergence|divergent]].”

An *infinite series* is the sum of the terms of an infinite sequence.  The standard form of an infinite series of a sequence $\left\{a_n\right\}$ is given by the sum of its sequences from the start of the sequence, say $n=1$, to $n=\infty$, such that:
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left\{a_n\right\} = S
$$
To approximate the sum, we use *partial sums*, or the sum of the terms of $\left\{a_n\right\}$, which we obtain through another sequence, say $\left\{S_k\right\}$, such that:
$$
S=\lim_{k\to\infty} \left\{S_k\right\}
$$
