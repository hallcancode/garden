Modular Arithmetic describe arithmetic of numbers that are bound to [[Introduction to Sets|set]] of modulus, where we write $\mathbb{Z}/n$.  

> [!info] Zero Factor
> A zero fact is a number that when multiplied by another non-zero number equals zero.  

> [!Info] Inverse of a Number
> The inverse of a number is another number that when both numbers are multiplied together equals $1$.  So, if $a$ is some number and $a^{-1}$ is the inverse of $a$, then we have:
> $$a \cdot a^{-1} = 1$$

As a general rule, we can not divide in modular arithmetic.  Division is just multiplication by an inverse.

> [!example] Example 1
> Given the following set:
> $$
> \mathbb{Z}/8= \left\{\left[0\right], \left[1\right], \left[2\right], \left[3\right], \left[4\right], \left[5\right], \left[6\right], \left[7\right] \right\}
> $$
> Solve the following: 
> $$\begin{align}3\left[x\right] + \left[2\right] &= \left[4\right]\\\left[3\right]\left[x\right] + \left[2\right] &= \left[4\right]\\\left[3x\right] + \left[2\right] &= \left[4\right]\\\left[3x\right]&= \left[4\right]- \left[2\right] \\&= \left[4- 2\right] \\&= \left[2\right] \\\left[x\right] &= \left[2\right] \cdot \left[3\right]^{-1}\\\end{align}$$
> We know that $\left[3\right]\left[3\right]=\left[9\right]=\left[1\right]$, thus the inverse of $\left[3\right]$ is just $\left[3\right]$, therefore:
> $$
> \left[x\right] = \left[2\right] \cdot \left[3\right]\\=\left[2 \cdot 3\right]=\left[6\right]
> $$
