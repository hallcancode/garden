Trees are bought and measured by [[Volume|volume]], specifically within board feet (12 x 12 x 1 in), trees and forests are important because they provide homes for wildlife, absorb harmful gases, store carbon, and provide wood for products (ie. homes, paper.)  Trees are a renewable natural resource.

Forests are ecosystems dominated by trees and other woody vegetation.  They cover large areas and support diverse plants, animals, and microbial life.  Forests provide ecosystem services:

 - Clean air 
 - Clean water
 - Carbon storage
 - Wildlife habitat
 - Timber
 - Recreation

Forests cover about 10.01 billion acres worldwide, that's about 31% of the planet.  Now within the United States, 29% of the total land area, and two-thirds of the forests are classified for commercial purposes.  In fact, only 18% is classified as national forest, and 10% owned by other federal agencies.  Specific to Idaho, about 40% of the state is forestland, ten key species:

 - Douglas-fir
 - Ponderosa Pine
 - Grand Fir
 - Western Larch
 - Sub Alpine Fir
 - Lodgepole Pine
 - Engelman Spruce
 - Western White Pine
 - Hemlock
 - Western Red Cedar

Forest stewardship is the responsible management and care of an ecosystem's health, productivity, sustainability, and biodiversity.  Within Idaho, stewardess must follow the Forest Practices Act (FPA) using Best Management Practices (BMP's.)  Key activities include:

 - Sustainable harvesting
 - Wildlife protection
 - Fire risk reduction
 - Reforestation
 - Restoration

There is an increased demand for proper stewardess from an increase in threats: climate change, invasive species, improper land use, and fire.  Current major challenges within Idaho are forest fire, drought, and bark beetle (pests).  The drought brings in more pests (which can smell stressed out trees), which kills and dries out trees, and then fire burns and starts faster.  

Fire used to be a very natural part of the ecosystem, and then, through marketing campaigns, like smokey the bear, taught the public that fire was harmful during a period when we thought not having fire was a good thing.  In fact, Idaho used to be covered in pines, like the ponderosa pine which has a tap-root, and other trees that were adapted to fire.  Today, through fire exclusion, weaker trees, like furs, have replaced the pines.

The reintroduction of fire is an incredibly slow process.  There are three growth stages:
 
- **Disturbance**, natural or man-made.
- **Seedlings**, natural or artificial regeneration.
- **Saplings**
- **Pole** 
- **Mature trees**
- **Decline**

*Shade tolerance* is a tree's ability to develop within the shade and competition of other trees, but there isn't a "shade loving tree."  Forest development typically moves from less shade tolerant trees, which self prune, to more shade tolerant trees, which have lower branches.
