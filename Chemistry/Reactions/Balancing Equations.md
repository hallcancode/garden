The central idea around balancing a chemical equation is the [[Law of Conservation of Mass]], which is related to the fact that [[Introduction to Matter|matter]] can neither be created nor destroyed.  This means that balancing equation is a simple concept, but that it can get very complication fast.

Some equations are easy to balance "by inspection," but others require us to fill in unknown variables and solve for them, example #1 outlines this tactic with a still simple equation.


> [!Example] Example #1
> Consider the following chemical equation:
> $$\ce{H2}+\ce{O2}\to\ce{H2O}$$
> This is an unbalanced equation, we can fill in three unknown variables:
> $$a\ce{H2}+b\ce{O2}=c\ce{H2O}$$
> From there we can create an algebraic system of equations:
> $$\begin{align}a&=c\\2b&=c\end{align}$$
> Solving for this, we can find that:
> $$\begin{align}\ce{1H2}+\frac12\ce{O2}&\to1\ce{H2O}\\2\times\ce{1H2}+\frac12\ce{O2}&\to1\ce{H2O}\\\ce{2H2}+\ce{O2}&\to2\ce{H2O}\end{align}$$

Additionally, here is a more complex example of a chemical equation:

> [!Example] Example #2
> Consider the following equation:
> $$\ce{Ca5(PO4)3(OH)}+\ce{H3PO}+\ce{H2O}\to\ce{Ca(H2PO4)2} \cdot \ce{H2O}$$
> We can start balancing things now:
> $$\begin{align}
> \ce{Ca5(PO4)3(OH)}+\ce{H3PO}+\ce{H2O}&\to5\ce{Ca(H2PO4)2} \cdot \ce{H2O}\\
> \ce{Ca5(PO4)3(OH)}+7\ce{H3PO}+\ce{H2O}&\to5\ce{Ca(H2PO4)2} \cdot \ce{H2O}\\
> \ce{Ca5(PO4)3(OH)}+7\ce{H3PO}+4\ce{H2O}&\to5\ce{Ca(H2PO4)2} \cdot 4\ce{H2O}\\
> \end{align}$$
