Stoichiometry is the measurement of the relationships between the quantities of reactants and products of a relationship throughout the process of a [[Introduction to Reactions|chemical reaction]].  The foundation of stoichiometry is founded in the [[Law of Conservation of Mass]], stating that the total mass of the reactants $m_0$ and products $m_f$ must be equivalent: 
$$m_0=m_f$$
One of the most basic stoichiometry relationships is the molar ratio, which relates the moles of any two substances within a relationship.  

> [!example] Example #1
> **Question.** How many moles of $\ce{I2}$ are required to react with $0.429 \text{ mol}$ of $\ce{Al}$ in the following reaction: 
> $$\ce{2Al + 3I2 \longrightarrow 3AlI3}$$
> 
> **Solution.** First, we will determine the molar ratio between the two reactants, which ends up being $\frac32$.  Now, we can multiple the known amount of aluminum: 
> $$
> 0.429\text{ mol }\ce{Al} \times \frac{3 \text{ mol }\ce{I2}}{2 \text{ mol }\ce{Al}} \approx 0.644\text{ mol }\ce{I2}
> $$

> [!example] Example #2
> **Question.**  How many moles of $\ce{Ca\left(OH\right)2}$ are required to react with $1.36 \text{ mol}$ of $\ce{H3PO4}$ to produce $\ce{Ca3\left(PO4\right)2}$ in the following reaction:  
> $$\ce{3Ca(OH)2 + 2H3PO4 \longrightarrow Ca3(PO4)2 + 6H2O}$$
> 
> **Solution.**  First we determine the molar ratio, which is $\frac32$ again, we know that we have $1.36 \text{ mol}$ of $\ce{H3PO4}$, thus:
> $$
> 1.36\text{ mol }\ce{H3PO4} \times \frac{3\text{ mol }\ce{Ca\left(OH\right)2}}{2\text{ mol }\ce{H3PO4}} \approx 2.04\text{ mol }\ce{Ca\left(OH\right)2}
> $$

> [!Example] Example #3
> **Question.**  How much, in grams, of $\ce{Ga2O3}$ can be prepared from $29.0\text{ g}$ of $\ce{Ga}$ within the reaction: 
> $$\ce{4Ga + 3O2 \longrightarrow 2Ga2O3}$$
> **Solution.**  We know that we have the molar ratio of $\frac24$ between $\ce{Ga2O3}$ and $\ce{Ga}$.  We can convert the mass to [[The Atom|atoms]]: 
> $$29.0\text{ g }\ce{Ga} \times \frac{1\text{ mol }\ce{Ga}}{69.723\text{ g }\ce{Ga}}\approx0.416\text{ mol }\ce{Ga}$$
> Now we can use our molar ratio:
> $$0.416\text{ mol }\ce{Ga} \times \frac{2\text{ mol }\ce{Ga2O3}}{4\text{ mol }\ce{Ga}}=0.208\text{ mol }\ce{Ga2O3}$$
> Finally, we can convert moles back into grams: 
> $$0.208\text{ mol }\ce{Ga2O3} \times \frac{69.723\left(2\right) + 15.999\left(3\right) \text{ g }\ce{Ga2O3}}{1\text{ mol}} \approx 38.988\text{ g}$$
