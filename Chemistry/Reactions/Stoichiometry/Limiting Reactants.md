The limiting reactant of a [[Introduction to Reactions|reaction]] is the reactant that there isn’t enough of to fully use the other reactants.  The general formula for determining the limiting reactant and how much product can be produced is as follows:

1. Determine the number of moles of each reactant.  
2. Compare the mole ratio of the reactants and the chemical equation.  If the ratio is exact there is no limiting reactant, if the ratio is too big then the reactant on the bottom is the limiting reactant, and if it is too small the reactant on top is the limiting reactant.  
3. Using the molar ratio between the limiting reactant and desired product, calculate the number of moles of product produced.  
4. Optionally, convert this final number into the mass.  

## Theoretical & Actual Yield
The theoretical maximum amount of product that we can yield from a chemical reaction is known as the theoretical yield.  Theoretical yield is based on the limiting reagent.  Actual yield is the true amount of product that we recover.  Like theoretical yield, actual yield is based on the limiting reagent.  Percent yield is the ratio between actual yield and theoretical yield, and rarely exceeds 99%.

The more steps that the experiment takes, the less the percent yield typically is.  In some cases, the actual percent yield exceeds 100%, this means that there is some impurity within the sample.
