Empirical formulas describe the relative ratio of the [[The Atom|atoms]] present within a [[Bonding to Form Molecules & Compounds|compound]].  More specifically, empirical formulas describe the ratio of moles of an atom.

> [!Example] Example #1
> **Question.** Water, $\ce{H2O}$, is comprised of two hydrogen and one oxygen, what is the relationship between the amount of each substance?
> **Solution.** Within 1 mole of water ($\ce{H2O}$), there are 2 moles of hydrogen ($\ce{H}$) and 1 mole of oxygen ($\ce{O}$).

> [!Example] Example #2
> **Question.** Mercury ($\ce{Hg}$) forms a compound with chlorine ($\ce{Cl}$) that is 73.9% mercury and 26.1% chlorine by mass. What is the empirical formula?
> **Solution.** Suppose that we have $100$ grams of our substance.  Then we have:
> $$73.9\text{ g }\ce{Hg}\times\frac{1}{200.59\text{ g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}}=0.368\text{ mol }\ce{Hg}$$
> And:
> $$26.1\text{ g }\ce{Cl}\times\frac{1}{35.45\text{ g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}}=0.736\text{ mol }\ce{Cl}$$
> Now, we can find the molar ratio between the two substances: 
> $$
> \frac{0.736\text{ mol }\ce{Cl}}{0.368\text{ mol }\ce{Hg}}=2
> $$
> Thus, we have: $\ce{HgCl2}$

> [!Example]  Example #3
> **Question.** Vitamin C, ascorbic acid, contains 40.92% carbon ($\ce{C}$), 4.58% hydrogen ($\ce{H}$), and 54.50% oxygen ($\ce{O}$), by mass.  The molecular mass is determined to be 176 amu, what are the chemical and empirical formulas of ascorbic acid?
> **Solution.** Assume that we have a $100$ gram sample of ascorbic acid, then we have:
> $$40.92\text{ g }\ce{C}\times\frac{1\text{ mol}}{12.011\text{ g}}=3.407\text{ mol }\ce{C}$$
> $$4.58\text{ g }\ce{H}\times\frac{1\text{ mol}}{1.0080\text{ g}}=4.54\text{ mol }\ce{H}$$
> $$54.50\text{ g }\ce{O}\times\frac{1\text{ mol}}{15.9999\text{ g}}=3.406\text{ mol }\ce{O}$$
> Now, we can find the molar ratio between all atoms:
> $$\frac{3.407\text{ mol }\ce{C}}{3.406\text{ mol }\ce{O}}\approx1$$
> $$\frac{4.54\text{ mol }\ce{H}}{3.406\text{ mol }\ce{O}}\approx\frac43$$
> $$\frac{3.406\text{ mol }\ce{O}}{3.406\text{ mol }\ce{O}}=1$$
> Finally, we can find the empirical formula by making all of these numbers integers to get: $\ce{C3H4O3}$.  Given that the molecular mass of the empirical formula is $88.062$ amu we can find the ratio between that and ascorbic acid’s mass to get:
> $$\frac{176}{88.062}\approx2$$
> So, we can multiple the empirical formula to get the chemical formula of ascorbic acid:
> $$2\left(\ce{C3H4O3}\right)=\ce{C6H8O6}$$

Notice how, through just a simple ratio, we can derive a lot of information about a chemical.  

> [!Example] Example #4
> **Question.** Determine the empirical formula for isopropyl alcohol, which contains only carbon $\ce{C}$, hydrogen $\ce{H}$, and oxygen $\ce{O}$, if the combustion of $0.255$ grams of isopropyl alcohol produces $0.561$ grams of $\ce{CO2}$ and $0.306$ grams of $\ce{H2O}$. 
> **Solution.**  First we will find the amount of substance within the produced material:
> $$0.561\text{ g }\ce{CO2}\times\frac{1\text{ mol}}{44.009\text{ g}}=0.0128\text{ mol }\ce{CO2}$$
> $$0.306\text{ g }\ce{H2O}\times\frac{1\text{ mol}}{17.01\text{ g}}=0.017\text{ mol }\ce{H2O}$$
> Now, we can figure out how much carbon and hydrogen are within these products:
> $$0.0128\text{ mol }\ce{CO2} \times \frac{1 \text{ mol }\ce{C}}{1 \text{ mol }\ce{CO2}} \times \frac{12.011\text{ g }\ce{C}}{1\text{ mol }\ce{C}}=0.154\text{ g }\ce{C}$$
> $$0.017\text{ mol }\ce{H2O} \times \frac{2 \text{ mol }\ce{H}}{1 \text{ mol }\ce{H2O}} \times \frac{1.0080\text{ g }\ce{H}}{1\text{ mol }\ce{H}}=0.034\text{ g }\ce{H}$$
> This means that the remaining mass must be the mass of oxygen, assuming that we have a pure sample, thus: 
> $$0.255\text{ g}-0.154\text{ g }\ce{C}-0.034\text{ g }\ce{H}=0.067\text{ g }\ce{O}$$
> With this information we can convert each back to the amount of each substance:
> $$0.067\text{ g }\ce{O} \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{15.999\text{ g }\ce{O}}=0.0042\text{ mol }\ce{O}$$
> $$0.154\text{ g }\ce{C} \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{12.011\text{ g }\ce{C}}=0.0128\text{ mol }\ce{C}$$
> $$0.034\text{ g }\ce{H} \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{1.0080\text{ g }\ce{H}}=0.0340\text{ mol }\ce{H}$$
> Finally, we can calculate the molar ratios of each substance:
> $$\frac{0.0042}{0.0042}=1\ce{O}$$
> $$\frac{0.0128}{0.0042}=3\ce{C}$$
> $$\frac{0.0340}{0.0042}=8\ce{H}$$
> Thus, we have: $\ce{C3H8O}$.
