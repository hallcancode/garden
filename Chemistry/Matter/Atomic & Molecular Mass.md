The mass of an [[The Atom|atom]] is called its atomic mass, and is measured in unified mass units ($\text{u}$).  The atomic mass, $m$, of an element $\ce{X}$ is approximately equal to the sum of the number of protons, $p$, and the number of neutrons, $n$, such that:
$$
m\left(\ce{X}\right) \approx p + n
$$
> [!Example] Example 1
> Hydrogen typically has $1$ proton and, in its most abundant state, $0$ neutrons, thus:
> $$m\left(\ce{H}\right)\approx1+0=1$$

Although, the molecular mass is the weighted average ([[Mean|mean]]) of all isotopes of a particular element.  The molar mass $M$ of a particular element $\ce{X}$ is proportional to the mass of a given isotope $m_i$ and the abundance of that isotope $a_i$, such that:
$$
M\left(\ce{X}\right)=\frac{\sum{m_ia_i}}{\sum {a_i}}
$$
> [!Example] Example 2
> Hydrogen has two isotopes, typically denoted as $^1_1\ce{H}$ ($1$ mass unit, $1$ proton) and $^2_1\ce{H}$ ($2$ mass units, $1$ proton) which are measured to $99.99\%$ and $0.001\%$ respectively.  Thus, the molecular mass $M\left(\ce{H}\right)$ is:
> $$
M\left(\ce{H}\right)=\frac{1.007\left(0.999\right)+2.014\left(0.001\right)}{1}\approx{1.008}
$$
