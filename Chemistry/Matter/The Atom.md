Matter is anything that has a [[Mass|mass]] and takes up [[Volume|volume]] (space).  The fundamental building blocks of all [[Introduction to Matter|matter]] are atomic particles.  

## The Structure of an Atom
Atoms are comprised of two separate components: a central core, called the nucleus cloud, and an outer cloud, called the electron cloud.  Most of an atom is filled with empty space, but, atoms are further comprised of three subatomic particles: electrons, neutrons, and protons, which all have their own properties: 

| Name     | Location                         | Charge ($\text{C}$)               | Unit Charge | Mass ($\text{amu}$)  |
| -------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------- |
| Electron | Outside nucleus (electron cloud) | $-1.602\times{10}^{-29}\text{ C}$ | $-1$        | $0.00055\text{ amu}$ |
| Neutrons | Nucleus                          | $0\text{ C}$                      | $0$         | $0.00727\text{ amu}$ |
| Protons  | Nucleus                          | $1.602\times{10}^{-29}\text{ C}$  | $1$         | $0.00866\text{ amu}$ |

Any two atoms with the same number of protons belong to the same element.  If two atoms have the same number of protons, but a different number of neutrons, then they are the same element, but of a different isotope.  When an atom has either a net charge (i.e., a non-zero charge; either positive or negative) that atom is called an ion.
