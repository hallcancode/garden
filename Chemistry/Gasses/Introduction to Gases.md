When the particles that make up a gases interact with the walls of their container the force exerted is called a pressure.  Pressure is inversely proportional to the [[Introduction to Classical Mechanics#Force|force]] excreted $F$ and the surface [[Area|area]] over which the force is applied $A$.  

$$
p=\frac{F}{A}
$$
Typically measured in PSI (pounds per a square inch.)  For gasses and liquids the gauge pressure is also directly proportional to the depth $h$, [[Introduction to Matter#Density|density]] $\rho$, and [[Gravity|gravitational field strength]] $g$, such that:
$$
p=h \rho g
$$

> [!tip] Standard conditions for temperature and pressure (gasses)
> The standard temperature and pressure (STP) conditions:
> $$\begin{align}
> p&=1\text{ atm}\\
> n &= 1\text{ mol} \\
> T &= 273\text{ K} \\
> V &=22.41\text{ L}
> \end{align}$$
> The final volume is also known as molar volume.  

## Boyle’s Law
Boyle’s law states the relationship between the volume $V$ and pressure $p$, such that:
$$
V\propto\frac1p
$$
Thus, the pressure and $p$ and volume $V$ are directly related to a constant:
$$
pV=k
$$
This also means that, due to conservation, we can relate the pressure of two different samples, of the same compound, such that:
$$
p_1V_1=p_2V_2
$$

## Charles’ Law
Charles’ law related the temperature $T$ (measured in Kelvin) and volume $V$ of a gas through a linear relationship:
$$
V=kT
$$
In fact, this relationship states that, when $T=0$ we also get that $V=0$.  We also get:
$$
\frac{V_1}{T_1}=\frac{V_2}{T_2}
$$

## Avogadro’s Law
Avogadro’s law states that when the volume $V$ and temperature $T$ are the same for any two samples of the same compound the number of moles are equivalent.  This means that Avogadro’s law states that number of moles $N$ and volume $V$ are proportional to one another:
$$
V \propto N
$$
