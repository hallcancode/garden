A theoretical gas that is composed of many randomly moving particles that do not interact with one another is considered an “ideal gas.”  Within our [[Introduction to Gases|introduction to such gasses]] we described a few different laws for the properties of gasses, we can combine all three to get:
$$
V\propto\frac{nT}{p}
$$
Where $V$ is [[Volume|volume]], $T$ is the temperature in kelvin, $p$ is the pressure in psi, and $n$ is the number of [[The Atom|atoms]] within the gas in moles.  We can derive a new law from this called the ideal gas law:
$$
\begin{align}
V&=\frac{RnT}{P}\\
PV&=nRT
\end{align}
$$
Where $R$ is a special gas constant.  The universal gas constant ($R$) can be approximated:
$$
R=0.08206 \frac{\text{L} \cdot \text{atm}}{\text{k} \cdot \text{mol}}=8.3145\frac{\text{J}}{\text{K}\cdot\text{mol}}
$$
