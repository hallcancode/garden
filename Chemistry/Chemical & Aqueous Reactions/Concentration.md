*Concentration* is how much solute is dissolved into a particular quantity of solvent or solution.  There are many ways of measuring concentration.  In some cases it's measured as a ratio of [[Mass|mass]]-to-mass ($m/m$) or mass-to-[[Volume|volume]] ($m/V$).  Some very dilute concentrations are measured in parts per million (ppm) or parts per billion (ppb.)

However, many Chemistry-related applications use a [[Moles|moles]]-to-[[Volume|volume]] ($n/V$) ratio, also called the [[Counting The Atom|molarity]], $M$.  The measurement for moles-to-volume concentration is typically $\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1}$, sometimes denoted as $\text{M}$.  Thus, the molarity $M$ of some system is inversely proportional to the number of moles of solute $n$ and total volume $V$, such that:
$$
M=\frac{n}{V}
$$
