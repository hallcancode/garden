When a chemical bond is formed between [[The Atom|atoms]] there are typically only a few types of bonds they can form.  The most basic ones are ionic, covalent, and metallic.  

## Ionic Bonds
An ionic bond typically forms when a metal atom looses an electron to a non-metal.  The change in electrons creates two ions, a positively charged non-metal (cation) and a negatively charged metallic anion.  Due to have opposite charges, the two ions are attracted to one another and form an ionic compound.  Generally, ionic bonds are very hard to be broken and have a large difference in electronegativity.  

## Covalent Bonds
When two atoms share electrons, called shared pairs (or bonding pairs), they form a covalent bond.  Covalent bonds typically form between two non-metals, or metalloids, and create what are called covalent molecules.  The difference in electronegativity of a covalent molecule is typically far lass than that of an ionic compound.  Covalent bonds tend to conduct electricity and be more malleable than other bonds.  

## Metallic Bonds
When several positive metal ions attract electrons they form a metallic bond.  Metallic bonds typically have the lowest difference in electronegativity, compared to ionic and covalent bonds.  

## Inter-Layer Bonds
*Inter-layer bonds* are weaker electrostatic bonds, more so than [[#Ionic Bonds|ionic bonds]].  Many [[Introduction to Minerals|minearls]] are formed through inter-layer bonds.
