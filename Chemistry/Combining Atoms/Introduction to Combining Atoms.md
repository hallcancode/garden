[[Introduction to Combining Atoms|Atoms]] can come together to form larger substances with different behaviors.  This can happen through the formation of either a mixture or a pure substance.  

## Pure Substances
A pure substance has a fixed composition as well as uniform properties, and, most importantly, pure substances are only comprised of a single type of particle, either an element or compound.  Pure substances are bound together, and can not be separated further through physical means.  The major properties of a pure substance include: 

 - **Fixed melting and boiling points.**  Pure substances will always melt and boil at the same temperature, whereas mixtures often have a range of temperatures at which they may melt or biol.  For an example, water ($\ce{H2O}$) will always biol at 100 °C and melt at 0°C.  
 - **Uniform composition and appearance.**  Pure substances look the same throughout, and this is because they are physically made of identical particles.  There is little to no difference in color, texture, phase, etc.
 - **Consistent chemical behavior.** The reactivity of a pure substance is always constant, unless contaminated or altered in some way, this means a pure substance has very predictable chemical reactions.
 - **Cannot be separated through physical methods.** Pure substances are identical elements or chemically bound, resulting in a very strong physical connection between all particles.  


> [!Example] Examples of Pure Substances
>  - Gold ($\ce{Au}$)
>  - Iron ($\ce{Fe}$)
>  - Oxygen ($\ce{O2}$)
>  - Water ($\ce{H2O}$)
>  - Salt ($\ce{NaCl}$)
>  - Carbon dioxide ($\ce{CO2}$)

## Homogenous Mixtures
If every component of a mixture is mixed so that there is no visible separate between the parts, then it is called homogenous, or a solution.  While homogenous mixtures look and feel the same throughout they also have:

 - **Uniform composition.**  Much like a [[Introduction to Combining Atoms#Pure Substances|pure substances]], homogenous mixtures look the same throughout.  
 - **Consistent properties.**  Every part of the mixture has the same properties as all other parts.
 - **Invisible components.**  All components of the mixture cannot be individually seen (i.e., mixture fully dissolves, mixture blends the individual substances.)
 - **Single phase.**  The mixture is all one single phase, solid, liquid, or gas.  

> [!example] Examples of Homogenous Mixtures
>  - Table salt dissolved within water.
>  - Sugar dissolved into filtered tea.
>  - Nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon making the air we breathe.  
>  - Copper and zinc forming a brass alloy.  

## Heterogenous Mixtures
When a mixture is not fully blended together and evenly mixed, it is called a heterogenous mixture.  Heterogenous mixtures have visible separation between the different components, meaning that they don’t look the same throughout, they also have: 

 - **Distinguished components.**  Separate components are easily seen.
 - **Inconsistent properties.**  Properties vary throughout.  
 - **Separation through physical means.**  Heterogenous mixtures are mixed evenly so they can be separated through physical means (e.g., filtering, decanting.)
