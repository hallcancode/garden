When an acid and a base react together, a very important class of reactions occurs.  Any acid will have hydrogen ($\ce{H}$) listed as the first element.  

> [!Example] Examples of Acids
> Some examples of acids include:
> 
>  - $\ce{HCl}$
>  - $\ce{H2SO4}$
>  - $\ce{H3PO4}$
>
>In fact, anything of the form $\ce{H_jX_k}$ is an acid, even water (which has the ability to sometimes act as a very weak acid.) 

As a general rule anything that can yield $\ce{H+}$ is an acid.  From there acids disassociate into [[Electrolytes|electrolytes]].  Bases, on the other hand, have the ability to make hydroxide ($\ce{OH-}$).

> [!Example]  Examples of basic acid-base definitions.  
> An example of an acid disassociating into electrolytes:  
> $$\ce{HCl_{(aq.)}\longrightarrow H^+ + Cl^-}$$
> As well as an example of a base making hydroxide: 
> $$\ce{NaOH_{(aq.)}\longrightarrow Na^+ + OH^-}$$

## The Brønstead/Lowry Definition of Acids (1923)
The Brønstead/Lowry Definition of Acids states that a molecule that is able to “capture” protons from the surrounding environment is a base, and molecules that “donate” protons to the environment are acids.
