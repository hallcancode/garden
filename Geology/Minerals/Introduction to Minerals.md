A *mineral* is a naturally occurring solid formed by geological processes with a crystalline structure and a definite chemical composition.  Minerals are the foundations of rocks, typically formed by [[Bonding to Form Molecules & Compounds#Inter-Layer Bonds|inter-layer bonds]]; graphite is an example of an interlayer bonded rock.  The most common rock-forming elements are: oxygen, silicon, aluminum, iron, calcium, potassium, magnesium, and sodium.  

## Formation of Minerals
There are three different ways to form minerals, which are:
* Crystallization from a magma.
* Precipitation from a hydrous solution, typically how salt forms.  
* Solid state diffusion, as a result of changes in pressure.  
These typically map fairly well to the three different types of rocks.  

## Types of Garnet
Garnets are useful for determining the original conditions of which a rock belonged to.  Some examples include:
- Pyrope garnet, following the formula $\ce{Mg3Al2SiO12}$.  

## Properties of Minerals 

* Color
* Luster, how the mineral reflects light.  
* Streak, the color that the mineral leaves when rubbed or turned into a powder.  
* Hardness, measured using Mohs Hardness Scale, to determine what the strength of a minearl is compared to other minerals.  
* [[Classical Mechanics/Statics/Fluid Statics/Density|Density]]
* Crystal form, also known as habit, is the shape that a crystal forms into when left to grow unconstrained.  
* Cleavage, the planes along which the mineral is prone to break regularly.  
* Fracture
