The [[Scientific Method#Theories|theory]] of [[Introduction to Plate Techtonics|plate techtonics]] states that there are three different types of plate boundaries. 

## Divergent Boundaries
*Divergent*, where the two plates are moving apart pushing the oceanianic crust outwards.  Divergent boundaries produce new ocean floor and crust.  For an example, the Atlantic Ocean plate boundaries diverge at about $1.7 \text{ cm} \cdot \text{yr}^{-1}$.  

*Early continental rifting* is caused by upwelling in the continental lithosphere, which typically produce early faulting and volcanos.  This activity causes thining of the lithosphere which leads to it dropping bellow the sea level, until the rift begins to produce new crust and spreads to an ocean.  

## Convergent Boundaries
*Convergent*, which recycles ocean floor, through a downgoing plate.  A true convergent boundary will have subducting lithosphere going down.  The subducting crust is the result of cooling after moving away from the divergent boundaries, making it more dense.  

*Subduction zones* are the most common convergent boundaries. They produce large earthwakes and tsunamis, which are produced by any verticle movement of the ocean floor.  

## Transform Boundaries
*Transform*, which neither creates nor recycles the ocean floor, instead having two plate translate between each other.  There are two different types of transform boundaries.  The most common of the two are oceanic transform boundaries, in these cases the ridges move realtive to each other because they produce oceanic crust at the same rate and in the same dirrection.  However, in the space between the two faults there are many earthquakes.  

Additionally, transform boundaries can form on land.  Transform boundaries on land do produce earthquakes at low depth, but can still produce eartquakes at a high magnitude.
