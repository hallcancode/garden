*Igneous rocks* are formed by the cooling of magma (melted rock.)  They are composed of [[Introduction to Minerals|minearl crystals]] that grow as the magma solidifies.  

*Intrusive rocks* form under the surface.  They form larger crystals that are visible to the naked eye and do not form via an eruption at the surface.  Intrusive rocks are typically of one of two textures: (1) *phaneritic*, where all crystals are visible, and (2) *pegmatitic*, which has very large crystals.  

Whereas *extrusive rocks* are igneous rocks that cool at the surface.  There are many different types of igneous rocks, which include: 
* Aphanitic, where crystals are too fine to be seen without a microscope.  
* Glassy, which does not have minerals present within it.  
* Pyroclastic, which are formed by material ejected into the air, such as through pumice or ash.  Very light rock containing air.  
* Vesicular, has void spaces formed by gas bubbles.
