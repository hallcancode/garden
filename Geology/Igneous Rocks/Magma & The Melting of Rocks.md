Typically, [[Introduction to Igneous Rocks|igneous rocks]] melt not through a change in temperature, but instead a change in [[Pressure|pressure]].  This is called *decompression melting*, which, when not modified in any way, results in basalt.  

*Flux melting* occurs when volatiles are added to the rock, such as $\ce{H2O}$, $\ce{CO2}$, $\ce{N}$, or $\ce{S}$, which lower the melting temperature.  This is similar to adding salt to melting ice or boiling water.  This is the primary process that is responsible for producing magma in subduction zones.  

*Heat transfer melting* is possibly the least common inside the Earth, which occurs from an increasing ambient temperature.  This is the least important mechanism for magma generation, but it still changes the composition of magma melt.  

## Magma Differentiation
*Partial melting* occurs when some minerals of a rock melt first, those that are lighter with higher silica content, while the others remain solid.  As the melted magma rises will change composition throughout its path upwards.  

During *fractional crystallization*, the first minerals to solidify will be the most mafic, meaning they have the least silica and the most magnesium and iron.  Effectively the inverse of partial melting.  

Magma differentiation can occur via several mechanisms, such as:
+ Mixing
+ Assimilation
